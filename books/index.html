Books

Zero to One

startups

Peter Thiel with Blake Masters

  • Made me think more carefully about whether an idea is actually different, or just a nicer version of something that already exists.
  • The monopoly framing stuck with me: build something specific enough that you can be clearly best at it.
  • Useful whenever I am judging a product idea and trying not to confuse competition with progress.

The Mountain Is You

self-development

Brianna Wiest

  • Helped me notice when I am the one making something harder than it needs to be.
  • Good for slowing down and asking what discomfort is actually pointing at.
  • Less of a tactics book, more of a mirror for patterns I would normally brush past.

Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind

zen

Shunryu Suzuki

  • A reminder that simple things are usually only simple when I stop overcomplicating them.
  • Useful for work because it pushes me to approach problems without acting like I already know the answer.
  • One of those books that makes attention feel like a real skill, not just a vague productivity idea.

Can't Hurt Me

discipline

David Goggins

  • The main takeaway for me was not to live like Goggins, but to stop negotiating with every small discomfort.
  • Useful when I catch myself treating tired, bored, or uncomfortable as a good enough reason to quit.
  • It raises the bar for personal accountability in a way that is blunt but memorable.

Value Investing: From Graham to Buffett and Beyond

investing

Bruce C. N. Greenwald, Judd Kahn, Paul D. Sonkin, and Michael van Biema

  • Helped make investing feel less like guessing and more like understanding a business piece by piece.
  • The focus on intrinsic value is useful outside investing too: what is something actually worth when the noise is removed?
  • Good mental reps for thinking about durability, competition, and whether an advantage is real.

Never Split the Difference

negotiation

Chris Voss with Tahl Raz

  • Changed how I think about hard conversations: slow down, listen better, and do not rush to fill the silence.
  • Calibrated questions are useful in work because they keep things collaborative without being passive.
  • The practical lesson is that people usually tell you more when they feel understood, not cornered.

The Comfort Crisis

resilience

Michael Easter

  • Made me more aware of how easy it is to optimize every bit of friction out of a day.
  • Useful reminder that discomfort can reset perspective, especially when work gets too screen-heavy.
  • Encouraged me to choose harder things on purpose sometimes, not because it is dramatic, but because it keeps me grounded.
