How H3 Spatial Indexing Works

A practical explanation of hierarchical hexagonal indexing and how I use it for location-based restaurant discovery.

Why location search needs an index

Location search gets expensive when every request needs to compare a user coordinate against a large restaurant catalog. The article will explain why an index lets the backend narrow the search before ranking results.

From coordinates to H3 cells

H3 maps latitude and longitude into hexagonal cells. In Krave, those cells are used as a practical bridge between raw coordinates and nearby restaurant retrieval.

Resolution and hierarchy

Different H3 resolutions trade precision for coverage. The full post will describe how cell resolution affects restaurant density, query cost, and result quality.

const resolution = 9;
const originCell = h3.latLngToCell(lat, lng, resolution);

Neighbourhood searches

Neighbourhood queries expand from a user cell into surrounding rings, then filter and rank restaurants by distance.

  • expand nearby H3 rings
  • query candidate restaurants
  • remove previously swiped restaurants
  • rank by distance and future personalization signals

How Krave uses H3

Krave uses H3 to keep the stack-generation path fast while a background ingestion system grows the restaurant catalog over time.

the goal is not just nearby search. it is nearby search that stays fast as the catalog grows.

Trade-offs

The final article will cover precision, sparse areas, boundary cases, and when direct geospatial queries are still the better tool.

smaller cells more precision more rings to search
larger cells simpler coverage less local detail
